# Dialogue

Sample scripts and the format the engine consumes. These scenes are written to
establish voice; they are not the full script, but they are meant to be usable
as-is.

## Voice guide

- **Vela** explains music with physical objects — water, walking, stone, bread.
  Never mystical, never condescending. Asks more questions than she answers.
- **The apprentice (Rill)** is funny when nervous and asks the question the
  player is thinking. Never stupid — wrong for interesting reasons.
- **Toma** is loud and physical. Short sentences. Teaches with the body.
- **Sable** is dry and withholding. Says the hard thing.
- **Quint** is warm and a little melancholy. Chords as places.
- **Bell** anthropomorphizes instruments.
- **Odile** speaks in cloth. Always correct.
- **Feld** is blunt and structural. Load-bearing walls.

**Rule:** no character ever says "as you know" or explains a term the player
has not already heard in sound. Sound first, word second — always.

## Scene 1 — The Threshold (Act 0, opening)

> The chamber is dark. A single instrument, badly out of tune, is audible
> somewhere below. VELA is seated. She does not get up.

**VELA:** You're the one they sent. Sit down.

**RILL:** I brought my— I have been practicing, I can play three—

**VELA:** Sit down.

> _Rill sits. Long pause. The out-of-tune note continues._

**VELA:** What do you hear?

**RILL:** A note?

**VELA:** Yes. What else.

**RILL:** ...A bad note.

**VELA:** Better. Why is it bad?

**RILL:** It's — it's flat? Or the string's old, it sounds sort of grey, it
sounds like a door that doesn't shut properly.

**VELA:** _(finally turning)_ Now say that again and notice that you told me
three different things.

**RILL:** I did?

**VELA:** You told me how it feels — grey, a door that won't shut. You told me
what it means — that something is wrong here. And you told me what it is: flat.
Three ears, all at once, and you didn't know you had them.

**RILL:** Is one of them the right one?

**VELA:** _(this is the line the whole game turns on)_ People who ask that
question usually end up with one ear and think they've been promoted.

> _CHOICE_
> — "So what do I do?" → **VELA:** You listen. Then you listen again, differently.
> — "Then what's down there?" → **VELA:** Something that used to be able to do all three.

**VELA:** Go down. Don't fix anything. Just tell me what you hear.

## Scene 2 — The Escapement (Act 1, Toma's first lesson)

> A chamber of stalled clockwork. TOMA is sitting on a gear, eating.

**TOMA:** Ha! They sent a small one. Good. Stand there.

**RILL:** What am I—

**TOMA:** Walk to me.

> _Rill walks._

**TOMA:** Stop. You did it already. You just don't know you did it.

**RILL:** Did what?

**TOMA:** _(stamping)_ Left. Right. Left. Right. That's the whole thing. That's
the bottom of everything down here. You've been doing it since before you could
talk. _(grinning)_ Now do it wrong.

**RILL:** Wrong?

**TOMA:** Walk with a limp.

> _Rill limps across the floor. The stalled gear twitches, once._

**TOMA:** _(delighted)_ There! You felt that? The gear felt it. You put your
weight where the floor wasn't expecting it. That's the good stuff. That's the
thing people dance to.

**RILL:** I just walked funny.

**TOMA:** You walked funny _against_ something. That's the trick — you need the
regular thing to push against or the funny thing isn't funny, it's just noise.
Take away the pulse—

> _The pendulum stops. Toma claps an irregular pattern._

**TOMA:** —and that's nothing. Nobody nods to that. Put it back—

> _The pendulum resumes. Same claps, now against the beat._

**TOMA:** —and now it's a groove. Same claps! Same claps, small one.

**RILL:** So the beat matters more when I'm not on it.

**TOMA:** _(long pause, then loudly, to the ceiling)_ Vela! This one listens!

## Scene 3 — The Long Stair (Act 2, Sable is not impressed)

> RILL has just completed their first melody commission.

**SABLE:** Again.

> _The melody plays._

**SABLE:** Again.

**RILL:** Is something wrong with it?

**SABLE:** Nothing is wrong with it. That is the problem. _(beat)_ Sing it back
to me.

**RILL:** I can't sing.

**SABLE:** You can't sing it back because you can't remember it. You wrote
sixteen bars and not one of them wanted to be anywhere. It walks up. It walks
down. It arrives home exactly on time, like a man who is afraid of his wife.

**RILL:** _(stung)_ It follows all the—

**SABLE:** It follows all the rules. Yes. _(softer, which is worse)_ Where is
its highest note?

**RILL:** ...The middle. Bar eight.

**SABLE:** And so the second half is the first half walking backwards, and by
bar twelve I know how it ends, and by bar fourteen I have stopped listening.
Move it.

**RILL:** Move the high note.

**SABLE:** Move it late. Make me wait for it. Make me think it isn't coming.

> _Rill drags the climax to bar eleven. The melody plays again._

**RILL:** _(quietly)_ Oh.

**SABLE:** Yes. "Oh." _(turning away)_ That is the only review I have ever
trusted.

## Scene 4 — The Vault (Act 3, Quint on cadence)

> A vast dark vault. A single warm light at the center. QUINT plays a chord and
> lets it hang.

**QUINT:** Do you feel that you're somewhere?

**RILL:** ...Yes?

**QUINT:** Good. That's home. Now — _(plays a distant chord)_ — where are you?

**RILL:** Somewhere else. It's not bad, it's just — I want to go back.

**QUINT:** That wanting is the whole of harmony. Everything else is furniture.

**RILL:** So I just go back.

**QUINT:** You can. _(plays a clean resolution; a door unbolts)_ There. Done.
Everyone's satisfied, everyone goes home, nobody remembers it.

**RILL:** What's the alternative?

**QUINT:** _(playing, and stopping one chord short — the door rattles and stays
shut)_ That.

**RILL:** _(actually startled)_ That's horrible.

**QUINT:** _(pleased)_ Isn't it. You leaned toward home and I moved it. Now
you're paying attention again. _(beat)_ Do it too often and you're just cruel.
Do it once, in the right place — that's the moment they'll tell their friends
about.

**RILL:** How do I know where the right place is?

**QUINT:** You don't, yet. You'll know when you've been homesick enough times.

## Scene 5 — The turn (Act 3 close, Vela)

> The third chamber lights. RILL is elated. VELA is not.

**RILL:** Three! Three chambers, and Quint said my progression was—

**VELA:** It was correct.

**RILL:** _(deflating)_ Everyone keeps saying that like it's a diagnosis.

**VELA:** _(sitting, heavily)_ Sit. I owe you something true. _(beat)_ The
Orchestrion didn't break.

**RILL:** It's a hundred years of dust and seized gears, of course it—

**VELA:** It has never broken. It answers people who understand it. It went
quiet because the last of us stopped being able to. _(beat)_ We got very good,
Rill. We could name any chord you played before the sound reached the wall. We
could take a symphony apart in our heads while it was still going. And somewhere
in all that naming, we stopped being moved by any of it.

**RILL:** ...You forgot how to like music?

**VELA:** We forgot that liking it was part of the work. We had one ear left.
The best one. The most expensive one. And this machine listened to us and
found nobody home.

> _Pause._

**RILL:** So when you keep making me switch how I'm listening—

**VELA:** I'm not being thorough. I'm keeping you from becoming me.

## Scene 6 — The Loomworks (Act 5, Odile)

**ODILE:** Two threads. Tell me which one is the cloth.

**RILL:** The top one. The tune.

**ODILE:** _(mutes the top line; the lower one continues, and it is beautiful)_

**RILL:** ...Oh, that one's good too.

**ODILE:** They're both good. That's the difficulty and that's the point.
_(beat)_ You've been weaving a pattern onto a backing all this time. A picture
on a sheet. This is different. Here, if I pull one thread out, the whole thing
falls apart in your hands — because there is no backing. They hold each other up.

**RILL:** How do I write two things that are both the main thing?

**ODILE:** You don't write two things. You write one thing twice and set them
walking at different times. _(a canon begins)_ Listen. Same thread. It just
left later.

**RILL:** That's cheating.

**ODILE:** That's four hundred years of cheating, and it still works.

## Scene 7 — The Nave (Act 6, Feld)

**FELD:** How long is your piece.

**RILL:** About ninety seconds.

**FELD:** Then you need a wall at thirty and a door at sixty, or they'll get
lost in it. _(kicks a block)_ People can't hold ninety seconds in their head.
They hold rooms. Give them rooms.

**RILL:** Isn't repeating myself lazy?

**FELD:** _(genuine surprise)_ Who told you that? _(beat)_ Repetition is how
they know where they are. You come back to the first room and they think:
I know this place, I've been here, I know what happens here — and then you do
something different in it, and now they're leaning forward. You can't surprise
someone in a room they've never been in.

**RILL:** So the return isn't the boring part.

**FELD:** The return is the only reason the middle was worth anything.

## Scene 8 — The Orchestrion (Act 7, finale)

> The full machine. All six chambers lit. Everyone present.

**RILL:** Do you want to hear it first? Before I put it in?

**VELA:** No.

**RILL:** No?

**VELA:** _(sitting down among the others)_ I'd like to hear it the way it's
meant to be heard. Once. With everyone. Not knowing what's coming.

**RILL:** What if it's bad?

**VELA:** Then it will be bad in front of people who came anyway. _(beat)_
That's the last thing I have to teach you and you're not going to like it: it
leaves your hands now. You made it. The machine will play it its own way — it
always does, that's what playing is. And then it belongs to whoever is
listening, and they will hear things in it you never put there.

**RILL:** That sounds awful.

**VELA:** _(smiling, eyes closing)_ It's the best part. Go on.

> _The player's composition plays in full. Each teacher reacts according to the
> element the player leaned on hardest._

## Dialogue format

Scripts compile to a data format the engine reads. Suggested shape:

```yaml
id: act1.toma.intro
speaker: toma
portrait: pleased
lines:
  - text: "Ha! They sent a small one. Good. Stand there."
  - text: "Walk to me."
    action: { type: await_player_move, target: toma }
  - text: "Stop. You did it already. You just don't know you did it."
choices:
  - text: "So what do I do?"
    goto: act1.toma.explain
  - text: "Then what's down there?"
    goto: act1.toma.deflect
    condition: { flag: heard_orchestrion }
```

Requirements this places on the engine:

- Speaker, portrait, and expression per line.
- Inline actions (play a passage, wait for player input, change the scene).
- Branching with conditions on flags.
- **Audio-aware lines** — dialogue must be able to pause for a musical example
  and resume after it, since every teaching moment is sound-first.

## Writing guidelines for remaining scenes

1. **Sound before terminology.** The player hears syncopation for a full minute
   before anyone says the word.
2. **Teachers disagree.** Sable thinks Toma is a barbarian. Feld thinks Odile is
   imprecise. Disagreement teaches that these are craft choices, not laws.
3. **Let the apprentice be right sometimes.** By act 5 Rill should be correcting
   people, and the teachers should concede.
4. **Keep Vela offstage during lessons.** She frames and reframes; the
   specialists teach. Her scarcity is what makes act 7 land.
5. **No praise for correctness alone.** Every teacher praises noticing.
